01 / OUR STORYA PEOPLE BUSINESS. IN EVERY SENSE.

Not just filling seats.
Building futures.

Qualified drivers can’t get a foot in the door because their paperwork isn’t in order. Employers take on the wrong person because nobody verified properly. Experienced workers spend twenty years in a role without a single qualification to show for it.

We built Citizen to close that gap — and we haven’t found a reason to stop.

Who we are.

Citizen Human Capital is a South African human capital partner headquartered in Johannesburg. We recruit and place qualified truck drivers, run outsourced HR functions for growing companies, and turn hard-earned workplace experience into formally recognised qualifications through Recognition of Prior Learning and Continued Professional Development.

We are not a generalist staffing agency that happens to do transport. The road is where we started, and it remains the sector we know best — licence codes, PrDP requirements, medical fitness, long-haul fatigue rules, shift cycles, the Road Freight Bargaining Council and the specific compliance burden that comes with placing people behind the wheel of a 34-tonne vehicle.

Our HR and qualifications practices grew out of that same sector knowledge. When your clients are transport operators, you learn very quickly that a missing employment contract or an expired medical certificate isn’t an administrative inconvenience — it’s a legal exposure. So we built our HR outsourcing and skills development services to the same standard we apply to driver placement: verify everything, document everything, communicate plainly.

02 / OUR PURPOSEPRINCIPLES, NOT POSTERS.

Capability recognised.
Compliance respected.
People treated as people.

We exist to make sure that the people who keep South Africa’s supply chains moving are treated as professionals — not as interchangeable bodies on a shift roster.

These are not values we wrote on a wall and forgot about. They are the rules we use to make decisions when the pressure is on — when a client needs twelve drivers by Friday, when a CCMA date is looming, when a candidate’s documents don’t quite match their experience.

01 ↗

Dignity is not
a soft skill.

The people we place are skilled professionals operating heavy machinery in dangerous conditions for long hours. They deserve correct pay, lawful contracts, valid medical cover and a recruiter who calls them by their name, not their licence code.

Every process we design starts with the question: would this feel fair if I were the person on the other end of it?

02 ↗

Compliance protects people, not just companies.

The Labour Relations Act, the Basic Conditions of Employment Act, POPIA and the Occupational Health and Safety Act are not obstacles to work around. They are the minimum standard for treating workers decently.

Watertight contracts, correct employment status and complete verification files protect the driver as much as the employer. An inspection should be a non-event, not a panic.

03 ↗

Experience deserves
a certificate.

South Africa has thousands of drivers and transport workers who can do the job — but who were never given the opportunity to sit in a classroom. RPL exists to fix that.

Fifteen years of safe, competent driving deserves recognition. Our purpose includes making sure the formal qualifications system recognises that reality.

PURPOSE IN PRACTICE

What this looks like
on a Tuesday morning.

It looks like a recruiter spending an extra twenty minutes helping a driver candidate scan his PrDP on a phone with limited data, rather than rejecting the application for a missing attachment.

It looks like telling a client honestly that their proposed contract terms will not survive a CCMA challenge, even though it might cost us the placement. It looks like refusing to pad a shortlist with unverified candidates just to hit a number.

Purpose is easy to write. We measure ours by the decisions we make when nobody is watching.

04 / OUR IMPACTLESS TALK. MORE LASTING CHANGE.

Sustainability.
The way we understand it.

We are a people business, not a mining house or a manufacturer. Our environmental footprint is small — an office, a few vehicles, a lot of laptops. We are not going to pretend otherwise.

But sustainability in South Africa means more than carbon. It means building an economy where skilled people can earn a living, where experience is valued, and where the companies that employ them can grow without breaking the law or burning out their workforce.

01PEOPLE & LIVELIHOODSSustainable employment,
not disposable labour.

The temporary employment services industry has a reputation for churning workers through short-term contracts with no benefits, no development and no security. We operate within that industry, and we are clear-eyed about the tension. Our approach:

  • We default to permanent placement where the role and the client’s operation support it. Labour broking is the right model for peak demand, seasonal work and trial periods — not for filling core fleet positions indefinitely.
  • Every driver we place on TES terms receives a lawful contract, statutory benefits (UIF, COIDA, leave), correct pay and access to our HR support line. Section 198 of the LRA is not a loophole; it is a responsibility.
  • Placement longevity matters, not just placement volume. A driver who leaves after three weeks is a failure, regardless of how fast we filled the seat.
  • RPL is a sustainability intervention. When we help a driver convert fifteen years of experience into a registered NQF qualification, we are giving that person a portable asset that no employer can take away. That is economic resilience at the individual level.
02SKILLS & THE SECTOR PIPELINEMore qualified drivers.
A stronger sector.

South Africa needs more qualified drivers. We are building the pipeline. The road freight sector faces a shortage of qualified Code 14 and EC drivers, compounded by an ageing workforce, high attrition and a training system that has not kept pace with demand. Our contribution:

  • RPL cohorts that convert experienced but unqualified drivers into formally certified professionals through structured assessment and evidence of workplace competence.
  • CPD programmes that keep existing drivers and supervisors current on fatigue management, dangerous goods, road traffic law and fleet compliance.
  • Employer-sponsored skills pipelines where we work with transport operators to identify high-potential staff, map their development needs and deliver structured qualification pathways that count toward B-BBEE skills development targets.
  • Learnership and apprenticeship support to create entry points for new drivers into the sector.

A sustainable transport sector needs people who are qualified, current and committed. Every placement, every RPL certificate and every CPD record contributes to that pipeline.

03GOVERNANCE & ETHICAL PRACTICEThe boring stuff
that actually matters.

Sustainability without governance is just marketing. Our governance commitments are specific and auditable:

  • Full legislative compliance across LRA, BCEA, EEA, OHSA and POPIA — not as a minimum, but as a baseline we build on.
  • Skills development with real outcomes. Our skills development spend is directed toward transport-sector qualifications that produce real employment outcomes, not certificates of attendance.
  • Ethical recruitment practices. We do not charge candidates for placement. We do not misrepresent job terms. We do not submit unverified candidates to clients. We do not discriminate on the basis of race, gender, age, disability or nationality in our screening processes.
  • Transparent reporting. Clients receive monthly placement reports, SLA scorecards and compliance documentation. We would rather lose a client than fudge a number.
  • Data protection. Candidate IDs, medical records, licence copies and employment histories are stored securely, accessed only by authorised staff, shared with clients only with explicit consent, and deleted when no longer required. POPIA compliance is an operational discipline.
HONEST BOUNDARIES

No glossy promises.
Just work that matters.

We are not going to publish a glossy sustainability report full of stock photos and vague commitments. We are not going to claim carbon neutrality for our office. We are not going to plant trees and call it a strategy.

What we will do is keep placing qualified people in safe, lawful, well-paid work. Keep turning experience into qualifications. Keep running HR functions that protect both employers and employees. And keep reporting honestly on what we’ve achieved and where we’ve fallen short.

In South Africa, that is not a small thing.
