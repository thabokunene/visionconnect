Case 01

Coega to Lusaka.

Six transformers. One corridor. Zero incidents.
Cargo
6x 80t transformers
Distance
3,200 km per unit
Duration
18 days per unit
Classification
Abnormal Load
The Brief

ZESCO needed six 80-tonne power transformers moved from Coega IDZ in the Eastern Cape to Lusaka, Zambia. A 3,200km corridor across two international borders and three provincial jurisdictions. SANRAL abnormal load permits required. Escort vehicle coordination at every stage.

The Challenge

Each transformer measured 4.2m wide and 4.8m high. Every bridge, overhead cable, and toll plaza needed a route survey. Beitbridge carried a 72-hour customs delay risk that would cascade across the entire convoy schedule.

The Execution

We deployed lowbed trailers with hydraulic steering. Escort vehicles pre-positioned at 48-hour intervals. A customs clearing agent embedded at Beitbridge for the full operation. All six units delivered inside a 22-day window, four days ahead of contract.

Case 02

Johannesburg to Lubumbashi.

Weekly reagents. Two borders. Full compliance.
Cargo
Mining reagents
Distance
2,900 km
Frequency
Weekly
Classification
ADR Class 8
The Brief

A mining house in the DRC Copperbelt required weekly shipments of corrosive reagents from Johannesburg. ADR Class 8. Two border crossings. Strict delivery windows tied to plant operations.

The Challenge

Corrosive cargo requires ADR tankers, tremcards, and emergency response plans at every stage. Kasumbalesa border post is one of the busiest in Africa. Delays there would stop production at the mine.

The Execution

We assigned a dedicated fleet of ADR tankers to the corridor. Permits pre-cleared for the full quarter. A customs agent at Kasumbalesa on permanent standby. Each weekly convoy runs with the same drivers, same route, same protocols.

Case 03

Free State to Beira.

Grain corridor. Seasonal surge. 8,000 tonnes.
Cargo
8,000t maize
Distance
1,800 km
Duration
6 weeks
Classification
Bulk Commodity
The Brief

A grain trader needed 8,000 tonnes of maize moved from the Free State to the port of Beira, Mozambique, inside a six-week harvest window. Side-tippers required. Weighbridge compliance at every load point.

The Challenge

Harvest windows do not move. The volume required multiple side-tippers running continuously. Beitbridge and Machipanda both needed bulk commodity clearance. Any delay would push cargo past the shipping deadline.

The Execution

We deployed twelve side-tippers on a rotating schedule. Load points ran 24 hours a day. Weighbridge certificates issued at source. Customs pre-cleared per convoy. Cargo arrived at Beira with three days to spare before vessel cutoff.

Case 04

Walvis Bay to Kolwezi.

Project cargo. 900 tonnes. Trans-Caprivi corridor.
Cargo
900t project cargo
Distance
3,100 km
Duration
45 days
Classification
Abnormal + Cross-Border
The Brief

A mining contractor required 900 tonnes of processing plant components moved from Walvis Bay, Namibia, to Kolwezi in the DRC. Multiple oversized pieces. Four border crossings. A fixed commissioning date.

The Challenge

The route crossed Namibia, Zambia, and the DRC. Every piece needed its own permit and route survey. Several components were over 5m wide. Road conditions on the final leg to Kolwezi were poor.

The Execution

We ran the project as a phased convoy over 45 days. Route surveys completed on all three national corridors. Permits secured in advance for each country. Two recovery vehicles assigned to the final section. Every piece arrived without damage.
